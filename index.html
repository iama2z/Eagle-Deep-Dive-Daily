Classroom Video Playbook: Teacher-Made & Student-Created Videos

Practical ideas from our staff meeting on making content-focused videos for students—and having students create their own.

Under 3 Minutes Rule
Track A • Teacher-Created Videos Screencastify / Canva / Loom

Making Short, Content-Focused Videos for Your Students

Students engage far more with a 2-minute video featuring your voice and your slides than a 15-minute stranger on YouTube. Use quick, unedited one-take recordings to clone yourself:

  • 1. The ‘Lab Setup & Safety’ Loop: Record a 90-second walkthrough of equipment setup and post it in Google Classroom so groups can re-watch at their lab tables instead of raising hands.
  • 2. The Worked-Example Replay: Record yourself solving one tough math problem or annotating one CER paragraph in OrbitNote so struggling students or parents can replay it at home.
  • 3. Missing-Student Slide Narration: Export your NotebookLM 5-slide catch-up outline into Canva or Google Slides and record a 2-minute voiceover for absent kids.
Pro Tip: Never re-record over a tiny verbal stumble—authentic, conversational one-take videos feel like real teaching and take 3 minutes total to produce.
Track B • Student-Created Videos 90-Second ‘One-Take’ Protocol

Having Students Make Videos to Prove Mastery

When middle schoolers have to explain a phenomenon on camera in 60–90 seconds, they process vocabulary and cause-and-effect at a much deeper level. Keep it structured with the 3-Box Paper Storyboard before they touch a camera:

Box 1 (0–15s)
The Hook & Claim

State the mystery question and your team's claim in one clear sentence.

Box 2 (15–60s)
Visual Diagram Proof

Point to your drawn model or lab graph and explain why using 3 unit vocab words.

Box 3 (60–90s)
Bust the Myth

Explain the #1 common trap people fall for and why the evidence disproves it.
